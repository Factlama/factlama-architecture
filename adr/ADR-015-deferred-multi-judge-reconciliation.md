# ADR-015: Deferred Multi-Judge Reconciliation

Status: Accepted

Decision: Multi-judge consensus/ensembles remain out of MVP scope, staying in Future scope as today. However, the v0.1 contract is shaped so that adding ensembles later is additive, not breaking: `provenance` carries an `attempts[]` array (one entry in MVP) rather than a single scalar provider/model/config, and a claim finding carries `contributing_judgments[]` (empty or single in MVP). Disagreement between judgments — including today's sequential-fallback case, where an initial and a retried attempt may disagree — is represented explicitly (a reserved `JUDGE_DISAGREEMENT` violation code and a `DISPUTED` state) rather than collapsed into `ABSTAIN` or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, which would misstate the reason. No design in the verification pipeline may assume exactly one judgment per claim.

Rationale: `CONTRACTS.md` and `domain-model.md` currently state a claim has exactly one final verdict, and `judge-provider.md`/`policy-engine.md` already promise that a fallback "records both attempts" — a promise the current scalar-shaped provenance cannot keep, so EPIC-04 would hit this contradiction the first time it wires a retry to a second provider, independent of whether ensembles ever ship. Separately, ensembles are the natural product answer to heterogeneous judge quality under ADR-004/ADR-006/ADR-010 ("your tenant's internal judge is weaker than a frontier judge" -> "run both in DEEP mode and surface disagreement"), so foreclosing the shape now would force a breaking change exactly when it becomes valuable. Building the ensemble behavior itself now would be scope creep against `PRODUCT_BOUNDARIES.md` and would substitute for the harder calibration work in ADR-010.

Consequences: `CONTRACTS.md` gains the `attempts[]` and `contributing_judgments[]` shapes at EPIC-03, before the wire contract freezes, at the cost of a nested object rather than a later major version bump. `verification-pipeline.md` gains a paragraph stating reconciliation is deferred and that no majority-vote or silent-suppression behavior may be introduced ad hoc. `mode` (`FAST|STANDARD|DEEP`) is later defined as a named, versioned routing profile including judgments-per-claim, with `DEEP` as the eventual home for ensembles; that binding is not made by this ADR.
